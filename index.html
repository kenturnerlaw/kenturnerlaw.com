CRIMINAL DEFENSE

WHAT HAPPENS AFTER AN ARREST?

If a loved one has been arrested, they will usually be transported to a local jail. You may be able to find information about their location, booking status, and bond on the county jail’s website.

At the jail, the person will go through the booking process. Jail staff will confirm the person’s identity, take a booking photograph and fingerprints, inventory personal property, and enter the arrest and identifying information into the jail system.

After booking, a person may be released after posting bond or held in jail. If the person remains in jail, the next step is generally a first appearance before a judge. This is not a trial and it is not the time to explain what happened.

FIRST APPEARANCE: YOUR RIGHT TO COUNSEL

The most important thing to know: You have the right to a lawyer at your first appearance. If you do not have a lawyer, ask the court to appoint one if you qualify.

What you should do

  • Ask for a lawyer if you do not already have one.
  • Wait to speak with your attorney before saying anything about your case.

What you should not do

  • Do not try to explain your side of the story in court.
  • Do not argue the facts of your case at first appearance.
  • Do not assume anything you say is “off the record.” It is not.

Important warning: Anything you say in court can be used against you later. First appearance is not the time to defend yourself or tell your version of events.

The first regularly scheduled court appearance after an arrest is called the first appearance. Under Florida law, a person who remains in custody generally must be brought before a judge within 24 hours of arrest. The hearing may take place in person or by video, depending on the court.

First appearance is usually a short, limited hearing. The judge will generally confirm the charges, determine whether there is probable cause to continue holding the person, decide whether the person can be released from jail, and set bond and any conditions of release.

Bottom line: Stay calm, say as little as possible, and ask for a lawyer immediately. Read more about what to do after an arrest or what happens at first appearance in Florida.
