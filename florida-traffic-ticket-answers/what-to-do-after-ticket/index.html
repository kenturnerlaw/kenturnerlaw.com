Florida Traffic Ticket Answers

What Should I Do When I Get a Traffic Ticket in Florida?

By Kenneth Ray Turner Jr. (“Ken Turner”), Florida Attorney

If you just received a Florida traffic ticket, the most important thing is simple:

Do not ignore it, and do not automatically assume that paying it is your best option.

Read the citation carefully and determine what kind of violation you were cited for, what deadline applies, and what options the citation gives you.

For many Florida noncriminal traffic infractions, a person who does not elect to appear generally must take the required action within 30 days after the citation is issued. Different procedures can apply to certain camera, automated-enforcement, criminal-traffic, and mandatory-hearing matters.

Step 1: Read the Entire Citation

Look at:

  • The alleged violation
  • The statute number
  • The date and location
  • Whether a hearing is mandatory
  • The clerk or court information
  • Any response deadline
  • The amount listed
  • Instructions concerning payment or a hearing

Do not rely solely on what you remember the officer saying on the roadside. The written citation matters.

Step 2: Do Not Miss the Deadline

One of the worst approaches to a traffic ticket is doing nothing.

Florida law establishes procedures and deadlines for resolving traffic infractions. Failure to properly address a citation can create additional consequences.

If you are uncertain about the deadline, contact the appropriate clerk of court or speak with an attorney.

Step 3: Understand What Paying Means

For many ordinary noncriminal traffic infractions, paying the civil penalty is not simply an administrative transaction.

Payment generally resolves the case as an admission of the infraction and waives a hearing on whether the violation occurred.

That may be perfectly reasonable in some cases.

In other cases, a driver may first want to consider points, traffic-school eligibility, the driver's existing record, commercial-license consequences, whether there is a basis to contest the citation, and whether the citation requires a hearing.

Step 4: Check Your Driving Record

A traffic citation should not always be evaluated in isolation.

A driver who already has points or previous violations may face different concerns than a person with a clean record.

Florida's point system can lead to license suspension when specified numbers of points accumulate within particular periods. Your existing driving history therefore matters.

Step 5: Determine Whether Traffic School Is Available

Some drivers may be eligible to elect a driver-improvement course for an eligible traffic citation.

Traffic school has rules and limitations, and it is not automatically the best choice in every situation.

Before making an election, find out whether you qualify, what deadline applies, what course is required, what the election does to the disposition, and whether another option might make more sense.

Step 6: Determine Whether You Want a Hearing

A driver may have the right to contest many traffic citations.

A hearing gives the driver an opportunity to require the government to establish the violation and allows appropriate evidence and legal arguments to be presented.

That does not mean every ticket should be contested. It means the option should be considered before it is waived.

Step 7: Pay Special Attention to Serious Citations

Some traffic cases deserve immediate attention.

  • Speeding 30 mph or more over the limit
  • Citations involving serious crashes
  • Certain school-bus violations
  • Criminal traffic offenses
  • Driving while license suspended
  • Reckless driving
  • Citations involving a CDL

Some of these matters are governed by procedures that differ substantially from an ordinary civil traffic ticket.

Do I Have to Hire a Traffic Attorney?

No.

A person can handle many traffic matters without an attorney.

The advantage of obtaining legal advice is that you can understand the available choices before committing to one of them.

If I review a traffic citation, my goal is to determine what the citation alleges, what consequences are realistically at issue, and what options make sense for the individual driver.
