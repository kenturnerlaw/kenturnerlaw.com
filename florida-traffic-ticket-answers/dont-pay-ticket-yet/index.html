Florida Traffic Ticket Answers

Don't Pay Your Florida Traffic Ticket Until You Understand Your Options

By Kenneth Ray Turner Jr. (“Ken Turner”), Florida Attorney

You received a traffic ticket.

The amount is printed on it.

The easiest response may seem obvious: pay it and move on.

Sometimes that is a reasonable decision.

But before paying a Florida traffic ticket, understand exactly what you are choosing.

For many noncriminal traffic infractions, paying the civil penalty amounts to admitting the infraction and giving up a hearing on whether the violation occurred.

Don't pay your traffic ticket yet. First, understand the consequences and your alternatives.

Paying May End the Case — But Not Every Consequence

A traffic case has more than one possible cost.

The immediate fine is easy to see.

Other considerations can include:

  • Driver's-license points
  • Driving-record consequences
  • Future license issues
  • Traffic-school eligibility
  • CDL consequences
  • Insurance considerations
  • The loss of the opportunity to contest the citation

Not every citation carries every consequence. That is precisely why the individual ticket should be evaluated before deciding what to do.

Option 1: Pay the Citation

Depending on the type of citation, one option may be paying the applicable civil penalty.

This may be the simplest choice for a driver who understands and accepts the consequences. The important point is to make the choice knowingly.

Option 2: Consider Traffic School

For an eligible citation and eligible driver, Florida law may permit an election involving an approved driver-improvement course.

There are restrictions on eligibility and frequency.

Do not assume that taking traffic school is always the best choice. It uses an election that may be valuable later, and the right decision depends on the driver's history and the citation.

Option 3: Request a Hearing

Many noncriminal traffic citations can be contested.

At a hearing, the government has the burden applicable to the proceeding, evidence can be considered, and appropriate defenses or legal issues can be raised.

The fact that an officer issued a citation does not eliminate the driver's right to use the court process.

Option 4: Speak With an Attorney

A traffic attorney can help determine which options are actually available.

  • What statute was cited?
  • Is it a moving or nonmoving violation?
  • Does the offense carry points?
  • Is a hearing mandatory?
  • Does the driver have prior points?
  • Is the driver eligible for traffic school?
  • Is the driver a CDL holder?
  • Is there evidence worth reviewing?
  • Can counsel handle the proceeding?

There is no single answer that applies to every ticket.

What If I Am Accused of Speeding 30 MPH Over the Limit?

Treat the citation seriously.

Florida law requires a mandatory hearing for specified speeding infractions involving an alleged speed 30 mph or more above the applicable limit.

That makes these cases materially different from an ordinary speeding citation.

What If I Already Paid the Ticket?

Contact an attorney or the clerk promptly if you believe you made a mistake.

Do not assume that payment can automatically be undone.

Available procedures depend on the case, timing, jurisdiction, and circumstances.

The Point Is Not “Never Pay”

I am not suggesting that every Florida traffic citation should be fought.

I am suggesting something much more basic:

Know what paying does before you pay.

The right outcome for one driver may be completely different from the right outcome for another.
