Florida Traffic Ticket Answers

Do I Need an Attorney for a Traffic Ticket in Florida?

By Kenneth Ray Turner Jr. (“Ken Turner”), Florida Attorney

A Florida traffic ticket may look like a relatively small problem. Sometimes it is. But paying a citation can also resolve the case in a way that affects your driving record, points, insurance considerations, or even your ability to drive.

That is why I generally recommend understanding your options before simply paying a ticket.

Hiring an attorney does not guarantee that a traffic citation will be dismissed or that a particular result will occur. What an attorney can do is review the citation, identify the applicable law and procedure, determine what options are available, and help you make an informed decision.

Paying a Traffic Ticket Is a Legal Decision

Many people think paying a ticket is simply paying a bill.

It is more important than that.

For many noncriminal Florida traffic infractions, paying the civil penalty is one method of resolving the citation. Florida law generally treats payment as an admission of the infraction and a waiver of the right to a hearing on whether the infraction occurred.

Before doing that, it can make sense to ask:

  • Will this violation result in points?
  • Am I eligible for traffic school?
  • Can I request a hearing?
  • Is there an issue with the citation or evidence?
  • Is this a mandatory-hearing offense?
  • Do I have a commercial driver's license?
  • Could this violation contribute to a license suspension?
  • Do I need to personally appear?

The answer depends on the citation and the driver's history.

What Can a Florida Traffic Ticket Attorney Do?

An attorney can review more than the amount printed on the citation.

Depending on the case, the representation may include:

  • Reviewing the traffic citation
  • Identifying the statute allegedly violated
  • Reviewing the driver's record
  • Determining whether a hearing is required
  • Requesting a hearing when appropriate
  • Reviewing available evidence
  • Identifying legal or evidentiary issues
  • Appearing at proceedings when permitted
  • Presenting legal arguments
  • Seeking an appropriate resolution
  • Advising the client about the consequences of available options

Some cases have straightforward solutions. Others require closer examination.

Can a Traffic Ticket Be Dismissed?

Sometimes traffic citations are dismissed. Sometimes they are not.

A dismissal can depend on the law, the evidence, the circumstances surrounding the stop, procedural issues, witnesses, and what happens at the hearing.

No attorney should promise that a ticket will be dismissed merely because the attorney was hired.

What is the best lawful strategy for this particular citation and this particular driver?

That is the question I focus on when reviewing a traffic case.

Florida Traffic Tickets and Points

Certain moving violations can result in points being assessed against a Florida driver's license after a qualifying conviction.

Points matter because accumulating enough points within designated periods can result in a driver's-license suspension.

The number of points and the consequences depend on the particular violation and the driver's record.

That is one reason a driver should understand the potential consequences before resolving a citation.

What About Speeding Tickets?

A speeding ticket can range from an ordinary civil traffic citation to a much more significant matter.

Florida law treats certain speeding violations differently. For example, qualifying violations involving speeds 30 mph or more above the applicable speed limit require a mandatory hearing.

Drivers facing unusually high alleged speeds should therefore pay close attention to the citation instead of assuming that every speeding ticket can be handled in exactly the same way.

Can an Attorney Go to Traffic Court for Me?

In many traffic matters, an attorney may be able to handle proceedings without requiring the client to attend every court event.

That does not mean a client can ignore a citation or assume that an appearance is unnecessary.

Whether the driver must personally appear depends on the type of case, the court's requirements, and the particular circumstances.

If avoiding missed work or travel is important to you, ask about this before the hearing is scheduled.

Should I Hire an Attorney?

You may want to speak with an attorney when:

  • You want to contest the citation
  • The violation could add points to your license
  • You already have points
  • You are accused of excessive speeding
  • A court appearance is required
  • You hold a commercial driver's license
  • You live outside Florida
  • Your license is already suspended or at risk
  • The citation arose from a crash
  • You are uncertain about the consequences of paying the ticket

Not everyone who receives a citation needs an attorney.

But everyone should understand what resolving the citation means before making the decision.
