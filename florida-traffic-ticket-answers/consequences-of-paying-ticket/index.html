Florida Traffic Ticket Answers

What Are the Consequences of Paying a Traffic Ticket in Florida?

By Kenneth Ray Turner Jr. (“Ken Turner”), Florida Attorney

Paying a Florida traffic ticket can resolve the immediate citation, but drivers should understand what payment means before doing it.

For many noncriminal traffic infractions, payment constitutes an admission of the infraction and a waiver of the right to a hearing concerning whether the violation occurred.

Depending on the violation and the driver's history, the disposition may have consequences beyond the amount of the civil penalty.

1. You Give Up the Hearing on the Infraction

When an eligible citation is simply paid rather than contested, the driver generally gives up the opportunity to have a hearing on whether the infraction occurred.

That means the driver should decide whether contesting the citation is worthwhile before payment—not afterward.

2. Points May Be Assessed for Certain Violations

Florida assigns points for certain traffic convictions.

The number depends on the offense.

Accumulating enough points during specified time periods can result in a driver's-license suspension.

A driver who already has points should therefore consider his or her complete driving history before resolving another moving violation.

3. Your Driving Record May Matter Later

Traffic convictions become part of the driver's history maintained by the state.

A single ordinary ticket may have limited practical significance for one person and be much more important for another.

  • A driver who already has several violations
  • A commercial driver
  • A driver approaching a point suspension
  • A driver whose employment depends on driving

The context matters.

4. Insurance Consequences Are Not Identical for Everyone

Clients frequently ask whether a traffic ticket will increase their automobile-insurance premium.

There is no single answer that applies to every person.

Insurance decisions can depend on the carrier, policy, driving history, type of violation, and other underwriting considerations.

A traffic attorney cannot promise what an insurance company will do.

The safer approach is to understand the driving-record consequences of the proposed disposition and, where necessary, ask the insurer about its own practices.

5. A CDL Driver May Have Additional Concerns

Commercial drivers should be particularly cautious about resolving traffic citations without understanding the consequences.

Federal and state rules applicable to commercial drivers can make certain convictions important even when the violation occurred in a personal vehicle.

If your livelihood depends on your commercial driving privileges, say so when speaking with an attorney.

6. Paying May Eliminate an Opportunity to Contest the Citation

There may be factual, legal, evidentiary, or procedural issues that are never considered if the driver immediately pays the citation.

That does not mean such an issue exists in every case.

It means the case has not been evaluated if no one has looked for one.

Should I Fight Every Traffic Ticket?

No.

Sometimes payment is the rational choice.

Sometimes traffic school is appropriate.

Sometimes requesting a hearing makes sense.

Sometimes hiring an attorney is worthwhile.

A good traffic-ticket strategy begins with the individual driver's circumstances rather than a universal rule.

What Should I Do Before Paying?

  1. What exact violation am I accused of?
  2. Does it carry driver's-license points?
  3. What does my existing driving record look like?
  4. Am I eligible for traffic school?
  5. Is a court hearing mandatory?
  6. Do I have grounds worth presenting at a hearing?
  7. Do I hold a CDL?
  8. Is the deadline approaching?

Once those questions are answered, the decision usually becomes much clearer.
