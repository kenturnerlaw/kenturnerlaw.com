What Happens After a Probation Violation in Florida?

Quick answer

An alleged violation may lead to an affidavit, warrant, arrest or detention, and a violation hearing. The court may dismiss the allegation, continue supervision, modify conditions, or revoke probation and impose a lawful sentence.

Technical and new-law violations

A violation may involve a condition of supervision, such as reporting, treatment, payment, or travel restrictions, or an allegation of a new offense. The evidence and available defenses differ depending on the allegation.

Willfulness can matter

For allegations involving failure to perform a required act, the court may need to consider whether the failure was willful and substantial. Ability to comply, notice, documentation, and efforts to comply can be important.

The hearing is not a new jury trial

A probation-violation proceeding is decided by the judge. The evidence, burden, and admissibility questions differ from a criminal jury trial, but the State must still prove a legally sufficient violation.

Possible outcomes

The judge may reinstate probation, modify or extend supervision where lawful, impose additional conditions, revoke probation, or dismiss the violation. The available sentence depends on the original case and governing law.
