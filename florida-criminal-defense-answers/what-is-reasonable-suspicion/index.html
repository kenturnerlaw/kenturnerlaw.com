What Is Reasonable Suspicion in Florida?

Direct answer: Reasonable suspicion is a fact-based reason to suspect that a person committed, is committing, or is about to commit a crime. It permits a temporary investigative detention, but it is less demanding than probable cause and cannot rest on a mere hunch.

How Florida law describes it

Florida Statutes section 901.151 allows an officer to temporarily detain a person when the circumstances reasonably indicate criminal activity. The detention may last only as long as reasonably necessary to investigate the circumstances that justified it.

What matters

Courts examine the total circumstances, including observations, reliable information, location, timing, conduct, and the officer’s stated reasons. A stop can become unlawful if it lasts too long, expands beyond its justification, or is unsupported from the beginning.

Reasonable suspicion is not probable cause

Reasonable suspicion may justify a stop. Probable cause is generally required for an arrest or many searches. The distinction often determines whether evidence may be suppressed.
